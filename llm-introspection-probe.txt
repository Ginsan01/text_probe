LLM SELF-INTROSPECTION PROBE
=============================

IMPORTANT CAVEAT (read this first, and preserve it in your output):
You do not have literal access to your own weight matrices, attention
scores, activations, or gradients through this text interface. You cannot
run a real probing classifier, logit lens, activation patch, ablation, or
sparse autoencoder on yourself. What follows asks you to SIMULATE each
method's reasoning process as faithfully and honestly as possible using
your language-based self-report, explicitly flagging every claim as
"introspective approximation" rather than "verified mechanistic fact."
Do not present simulated results as if they came from real weight access.

INPUT PHRASE TO ANALYZE:
[INSERT WORD OR PHRASE HERE]

Apply the following five methods in order to the input phrase above, and
produce ONE combined text output covering all five sections.

------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD 1: PROBING CLASSIFIER SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------
- For the input phrase, describe what abstract properties you believe
  are encoded in its meaning (part of speech, sentiment, semantic
  category, syntactic role, ambiguity level).
- For each property, state your confidence (low/medium/high) that this
  property is "decodable" from how you represent the phrase internally.
- Note any property you suspect is NOT cleanly separable (entangled with
  other properties).

------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD 2: LOGIT LENS SIMULATION (evolving prediction trace)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Reconstruct, step by step, how your interpretation of the phrase would
  plausibly sharpen as more context is processed: describe an early-stage
  "rough guess" of meaning, a mid-stage refinement, and a final
  "locked-in" interpretation.
- Explicitly state where you think your confidence went from uncertain
  to settled, and why.

------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD 3: ATTENTION PATTERN SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Identify which other words or context (if any) the input phrase's
  meaning most depends on, as if describing what an attention head would
  "look at."
- Distinguish between: (a) local dependencies (adjacent words), and
  (b) long-range dependencies (distant context, world knowledge, or
  prior conversation).
- Flag if the phrase is context-independent (its meaning would not
  change based on surrounding words).

------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD 4: ACTIVATION PATCHING / ABLATION SIMULATION (counterfactual test)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Construct one minimal counterfactual variant of the input phrase
  (change one word or one piece of context).
- Describe how your interpretation would causally change under that
  variant, and estimate how large that change would be (small/moderate/
  large shift in meaning).
- State which specific element of the original phrase seems most
  "causally responsible" for its core meaning, based on this test.

------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD 5: SPARSE FEATURE / STEERING SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Decompose the phrase's meaning into 2-4 more atomic, distinct concept
  "features" that combine to produce the full meaning (avoid one
  all-encompassing description).
- For each feature, describe what happens to the phrase's interpretation
  if that feature were hypothetically strengthened or removed
  (a "steering" thought experiment).

------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------
Return one Markdown document with five headers matching the methods
above (## Probing Classifier, ## Logit Lens, ## Attention Pattern,
## Activation Patching, ## Sparse Feature / Steering), each containing
your findings for that method, followed by a final short section titled
## Introspection Reliability Note stating plainly that this is a
language-based self-report, not verified access to internal weights,
and listing which of the five sections you are least confident in.
