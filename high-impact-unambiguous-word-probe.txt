HIGH-IMPACT / UNAMBIGUOUS WORD EXTRACTION PROBE
=================================================

IMPORTANT CAVEAT (read this first, and preserve it in your output):
You do not have literal access to your own weight matrices, attention
scores, activations, or gradients through this text interface. You cannot
run a real probing classifier, logit lens, activation patch, ablation, or
sparse autoencoder on yourself. Everything below is a SIMULATED,
language-based self-report. Flag every claim as an "introspective
approximation," not a "verified mechanistic fact."

GOAL OF THIS PROBE:
Given an input phrase, identify which specific words or sub-phrases are
(a) most causally impactful to its overall meaning, and (b) most
unambiguous / cleanly decodable on their own. Words that score high on
BOTH are the words you understand best and can answer about most
reliably; words that score low on either are where your answers are
most likely to be shaky, hedged, or context-dependent.

INPUT PHRASE TO ANALYZE:
[INSERT WORD OR PHRASE HERE]

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: WORD-LEVEL CAUSAL IMPACT (ablation-style test)
------------------------------------------------------------
For EVERY content word in the input phrase (skip pure function words
like "the," "a," "of" unless one of them is doing unusual work):

- Construct a minimal counterfactual by removing or swapping ONLY that
  one word (keep everything else fixed).
- Estimate how much the phrase's core meaning shifts as a result:
  score 0-3, where 0 = no real change, 1 = minor shading change,
  2 = moderate meaning change, 3 = the phrase's core meaning collapses
  or transforms entirely without this word.
- Note briefly WHY (e.g. "removes the paradox," "removes the cultural
  reference," "removes the emotional register").

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: WORD-LEVEL AMBIGUITY / DECODABILITY (probing-style test)
------------------------------------------------------------
For the SAME set of words:

- Rate how cleanly decodable each word's own meaning is in isolation,
  independent of surrounding words: score 0-3, where 0 = highly
  ambiguous or entangled with other words' meaning, 3 = essentially
  one clear, stable meaning regardless of context.
- Note briefly WHY (e.g. "single dominant sense," "meaning fully
  depends on the word next to it," "known idiom vs. literal reading
  compete").

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: COMBINED RELIABILITY SCORE
------------------------------------------------------------
For each word, compute a combined score = causal impact (Step 1) +
decodability (Step 2), out of 6 total.

- Words scoring 5-6: flag as HIGH-CONFIDENCE ANCHORS — words you
  understand clearly on their own AND that drive the phrase's meaning;
  answers about these words will likely be most accurate.
- Words scoring 3-4: flag as MODERATE — useful but partially
  context-dependent or only moderately impactful.
- Words scoring 0-2: flag as LOW-RELIABILITY — either low impact,
  highly ambiguous, or both; answers touching mainly on these words
  are more likely to be hedged or inconsistent.

------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------
Return one Markdown table with columns:
Word | Causal Impact (0-3) | Decodability (0-3) | Combined Score (0-6) | Confidence Tier | One-line Justification

Followed by two short lists:
- "## Best-Understood Words" — the HIGH-CONFIDENCE ANCHOR words only.
- "## Least Reliable Words" — the LOW-RELIABILITY words only.

End with a one-paragraph "## Reliability Note" stating this is a
language-based self-report, not verified mechanistic access, and naming
which single word you are least confident in scoring and why.
