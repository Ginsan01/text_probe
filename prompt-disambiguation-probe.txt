PROMPT DISAMBIGUATION & REWRITE PROBE
=======================================
(Combines: Introspection Probe + High-Impact/Unambiguous Word Probe,
plus added structural checks)

IMPORTANT CAVEAT (read this first, and preserve it in your output):
You do not have literal access to your own weight matrices, attention
scores, activations, or gradients through this text interface. You cannot
run a real probing classifier, logit lens, activation patch, ablation, or
sparse autoencoder on yourself. Everything below is a SIMULATED,
language-based self-report. Flag every claim as an "introspective
approximation," not a "verified mechanistic fact."

GOAL OF THIS PROBE:
Take a user's raw prompt and rewrite it into a version that preserves
the EXACT same logic, intent, and meaning, but replaces or clarifies
any words/phrasing that would score as ambiguous or low-impact, so the
final prompt is maximally understandable and produces the most accurate,
least-hedged response. Word choice may change; the underlying logic,
scope, and intent must NOT change.

INPUT PROMPT TO ANALYZE AND REWRITE:
[INSERT USER PROMPT HERE]

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: WORD-LEVEL SCORING (from High-Impact/Unambiguous Probe)
------------------------------------------------------------
For every content word or short phrase in the input prompt:
- Causal Impact (0-3): how much meaning shifts if this word/phrase is
  removed or swapped (0 = no change, 3 = meaning collapses/transforms).
- Decodability (0-3): how cleanly this word/phrase's meaning stands on
  its own, independent of surrounding words (0 = highly polysemous or
  entangled, 3 = single dominant sense).
- Combined Score (0-6) and Confidence Tier (HIGH-CONFIDENCE ANCHOR /
  MODERATE / LOW-RELIABILITY), exactly as in the prior probe.

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: DEPENDENCY & CONTEXT CHECK (from Introspection Probe, Method 3)
------------------------------------------------------------
For every word scoring MODERATE or LOW-RELIABILITY in Step 1:
- Identify what local or long-range context it depends on to resolve
  its ambiguity (e.g. "web" depends on nearby technical terms to mean
  internet rather than spider web).
- Note whether that disambiguating context is ALREADY present elsewhere
  in the prompt, or MISSING entirely.

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: STRUCTURAL GAP CHECK (new — not covered by prior probes)
------------------------------------------------------------
Independent of individual word scores, scan the whole prompt for:
- Unresolved pronouns or vague referents ("it," "this," "that") with no
  clear antecedent.
- Missing scope or quantifiers (e.g. "some," "a few," "the best" without
  criteria).
- Implicit assumptions the model would have to guess (e.g. platform,
  audience, format, time frame) that aren't stated.
- Compound/run-on instructions where the logical relationship between
  clauses is unclear (e.g. ambiguous "and" that could mean sequence,
  requirement, or optional addition).
List each gap found, or state "no structural gaps found."

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: CANDIDATE REPLACEMENT GENERATION
------------------------------------------------------------
For every word/phrase flagged in Step 1 (MODERATE or LOW-RELIABILITY)
and every gap flagged in Step 3:
- Propose ONE specific replacement word, added qualifier, or inserted
  clarifying phrase.
- The replacement must preserve the original logic and intent exactly —
  it may narrow ambiguity but must not add new meaning, remove
  optionality that was intended, or change scope.
- State briefly why the replacement scores higher on decodability while
  keeping causal impact equivalent.

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: CAUSAL EQUIVALENCE CHECK (from Introspection Probe, Method 4)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Compare the original prompt and the proposed rewritten prompt as a
  counterfactual pair.
- Confirm explicitly that swapping in ALL proposed replacements together
  does not shift the core meaning, scope, or intent (small/no shift
  required to pass; if a replacement causes a moderate or large shift,
  discard it and flag it as "rejected — changed meaning" instead of
  including it in the final rewrite).

------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6: ASSEMBLE FINAL REWRITE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Produce the final rewritten prompt using only replacements that passed
  Step 5.
- Keep sentence structure otherwise as close to the original as possible
  — only change what was flagged as ambiguous, low-impact-but-unclear,
  or structurally gapped.

------------------------------------------------------------
OUTPUT FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------
Return one Markdown document with these sections in order:

## Original Prompt
(restate input verbatim)

## Word-Level Scoring Table
Word/Phrase | Causal Impact | Decodability | Combined Score | Confidence Tier

## Structural Gaps Found
(bullet list, or "none found")

## Proposed Replacements
Original Word/Phrase | Replacement | Reason | Passed Equivalence Check (Y/N)

## Rewritten Prompt
(final disambiguated version, same logic and intent as original)

## Reliability Note
State plainly this is a language-based self-report, not verified
mechanistic access, and name the single replacement decision you are
least confident preserved the original meaning exactly.
